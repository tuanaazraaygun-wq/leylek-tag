# B5.4 — Phase 1: Logo DNA Analysis

**Sprint:** B5.4 — LeylekTAG Brand Studio  
**Study source (locked):** `frontend/assets/images/leylek-logo-premium.png`  
**Mode:** Design-lab only · production freeze  
**Date:** 2026-06-21

---

## Executive read

The LeylekTAG premium logo is not a mascot and not a generic bird icon. It is a **vertical stork presence** held inside an **orbital arc** — a symbol of guided movement, trust, and technological calm. Every element earns its place by answering one question: *Does this make the user feel they are inside a system that knows where they are going?*

B6-7 failed not because the geometry was wrong in code — but because vector simplification **stripped the emotional contract**: long beak authority, arc mass, one-leg elegance, and the metallic premium read collapsed into a flat startup glyph. This analysis explains **why** each element exists — not how it was drawn.

---

## 1. Silhouette

**What it is:** A tall, asymmetric profile — stork head and neck rising from a wide cyan arc base. The outer contour reads as one continuous gesture: arc → body → beak tip.

**Why it exists:** LeylekTAG is named for the stork (*leylek*). The silhouette must be recognizable at a glance without text — on splash, app icon, notification, and map watermark. The vertical axis signals **presence** (someone is here, the system is active). The arc grounds the bird so it never floats like a clipart sticker.

**Perception failure mode (B5.2/B6-2):** When body mass, beak length, and arc thickness compress, the silhouette becomes a rounded teardrop + thin ring — users read “new app icon,” not “my LeylekTAG.”

---

## 2. Optical weight

**What it is:** Visual mass concentrates at the **bottom third** (thick arc + body base) and releases upward into a sharp, light head. The logo feels anchored, not top-heavy.

**Why it exists:** Premium brands feel **stable**. A ride platform must communicate reliability before motion. Bottom-weight says: *the ground holds you*; the beak points forward into the journey. If weight shifts to the head or arc thins, the mark feels tentative — “startup experimenting,” not “platform you trust at night on a highway.”

---

## 3. Neck curve

**What it is:** A slender, slightly organic S-curve — not a straight pillar, not a cartoon bend. The neck narrows mid-span and widens subtly at the shoulder before the head.

**Why it exists:** The neck is the **personality bridge** between animal grace and digital precision. Too straight = corporate glyph. Too curved = mascot. The original neck says *elegant alertness* — a stork watching the horizon, not posing. Users who know LeylekTAG feel this curve even if they cannot name it.

---

## 4. Head character

**What it is:** Small, rounded cranium with a subtle crest tuft integrated into the profile — not a separate spike. The head is modest relative to beak length.

**Why it exists:** LeylekTAG is **confident but not aggressive**. The head is intelligent, not cute. A large round head would read childish; a sharp angular head would read predatory. The crest whispers *biological truth* without becoming a zoology diagram — enough to say “stork,” not enough to say “character sheet.”

---

## 5. Beak attitude

**What it is:** Long, horizontal mandible extending right — dominant linear element. Tip is sharp; upper line nearly level with a slight downward attitude at the tip.

**Why it exists:** The beak is **direction and intent**. It is the longest single stroke in the mark and defines LeylekTAG’s forward motion metaphor: *where you’re headed matters*. Shortening the beak (B5.2 drift) was the single highest-impact recognition break — the logo stopped “looking forward” and started “looking like every other minimal bird.”

---

## 6. Wing energy

**What it is:** A single thin secondary curve on the back contour — one stroke suggesting folded wing, not feather detail.

**Why it exists:** Without the wing line, the body is a static teardrop. The wing adds **kinetic memory** — this bird moves, carries, migrates. It also creates negative-space rhythm between body and void. Removing or merging the wing into fill loses the “living stork” read and flattens to icon template.

---

## 7. Orbital arc

**What it is:** A thick, open swoosh — nearly a U, not a closed circle. Heaviest stroke at bottom center; tapered, rounded terminals left and right with a gap at top-right.

**Why it exists:** The arc is LeylekTAG’s **technology signature** — orbit, coverage, GPS shell, protective field. It separates LeylekTAG from wildlife logos and from pin-drop maps. The open gap prevents “badge seal” government energy; the bottom mass prevents “loading spinner” energy. When the arc thins to a hairline, the brand loses its **scale and premium instrument** feeling.

---

## 8. Eye placement

**What it is:** One small cyan dot on the head — same hue family as the arc. Positioned upper-right on the cranium, non-expressive.

**Why it exists:** The eye is the **system alive** indicator — a single pixel of intelligence. It links bird to orbit (shared cyan DNA). It is not a face; it must never become large, white, or cartoon. Misplaced or oversized eyes trigger “mascot” rejection instantly.

---

## 9. Negative space

**What it is:** Deliberate void between wing curve and body; open arc gap; slender leg channel; black field defining beak underside.

**Why it exists:** Premium marks breathe. Negative space lets the stork **stand in** the arc rather than sit on it. Filling these gaps for “cleaner vector” (B5.2) merged leg into body and collapsed wing separation — the logo felt **over-simplified**, not refined.

---

## 10. Balance

**What it is:** Optical center slightly below geometric center — stork mass sits in the cradle of the arc; beak extends into upper-right quadrant for forward tension.

**Why it exists:** Balance encodes **calm readiness**. The bird does not tip; the arc catches it. For app icon safe zones and splash cropping, this balance must survive masking — otherwise the mark feels off-center in iOS squircle and Android adaptive masks. Constitution anchor (268, 278) exists because of this optical truth, not math pedantry.

---

## 11. One-leg stance (signature pose)

**What it is:** Thin vertical standing leg + tucked bent leg on the body left — classic stork rest pose.

**Why it exists:** This is one of LeylekTAG’s **strongest mnemonic devices**. Real storks stand on one leg; users subconsciously associate the pose with the brand name. Removing the tucked leg or thickening both into a blob erases “LeylekTAG stork” and replaces it with “generic bird blob.”

---

## 12. Material memory (perceptual, not production)

**What it is:** In user memory (from historical premium PNG), satin silver body with soft highlight — not flat white.

**Why it exists:** Material communicates **premium instrument** — like jewelry or automotive trim, not a free icon pack. Vector evolution may flatten color for export tiers, but silhouette and weight must preserve the *felt* premium or users say “logo cheapened.”
